# a96J06 reader-oriented telling

## Expert reference

**[B1]** Fix (r>0) and suppose, for contradiction, that
(\lVert p(z)\rVert\le r) throughout the radius-(r) disk.

**[B2]** Schwarz rigidity uses the unit disk, but the assumption has radius
(r).  Rescale:

\[
g(w)=r^{-1}p(rw).
\]

Then (g) is holomorphic on the unit disk, maps it to the closed unit disk,
fixes (0), and has (g'(0)=1).

**[B4]** The equality case gives, on the unit disk,

\[
g(w)=g(0)+(w-0)\operatorname{dslope}(g,0,0).
\]

Here (\operatorname{dslope}(g,0,0)=g'(0)=1) and (g(0)=0), so the affine
slope is exactly (1) and the formula reduces to (g(w)=w); no arbitrary
rotation factor remains.

**[B5]** Hence (p(z)=z) on the radius-(r) disk.  Agreement on this infinite
set makes the two polynomials identical.  Applying the degree-(N) coefficient
function to that equality makes their degree-(N) coefficients equal; these are
(c_N) and (0), since (N\ge2).  Thus (c_N=0), contrary to hypothesis.

**[B6]** The supposition is false.  Therefore every positive-radius disk
contains (\zeta) with (\lVert\zeta\rVert<r) and
(\lVert p(\zeta)\rVert>r).
