# b95J01 reader-oriented telling

## Expert reference

**[B1]** Let \(T=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}: g^{m}\in H\}\), the preimage of \(H\)
under \(m\mapsto g^{m}\); this packaging, rather than a least positive
exponent plus the division algorithm, is what makes the divisibility for all
integers fall out at once.

**[B3]** By the pigeonhole lemma on cosets, \(T\) contains a positive
integer, so \(T=a\mathbb{Z}\) for some \(a\ne0\).

**[B4]** With \(k=|a|\): \(g^{m}\in H\) if and only if \(k\mid m\), for every
integer \(m\).

**[B5]** So \(k\) is the least positive exponent with \(g^{k}\in H\) and
divides every \(m\) with \(g^{m}\in H\).

**[B6]** If \(H\) is normal, \(G/H\) has order \([G:H]\) and the coset
\(gH\) has order \(k\); Lagrange gives \(k\mid[G:H]\).

**[B7]** Equivalently \(g^{[G:H]}\in H\), and the criterion of part (a) at
\(m=[G:H]\) returns \(k\mid[G:H]\).

**[B8]** In \(S_{3}\), let \(H\) be generated by the transposition
\((1\,2)\): order \(2\), index \(3\), not normal.

**[B9]** For \(g=(1\,3)\) the least exponent is \(2\), and \(2\) does not
divide the index \(3\), so part (b) fails without normality.
