# b95J01 publication telling

## Publication

**[B1]** Let \(T=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}: g^{m}\in H\}\), the preimage of \(H\)
under \(m\mapsto g^{m}\).

**[B2]** Since \(H\) has finite index \(n\), two of the cosets
\(H,gH,\dots,g^{n}H\) coincide, so \(g^{b-a}\in H\) for some
\(0\le a<b\le n\).

**[B3]** Thus \(T\) is a nonzero subgroup of \(\mathbb{Z}\), say
\(T=a\mathbb{Z}\) with \(a\ne0\).

**[B4]** With \(k=|a|\), membership gives \(g^{m}\in H\) if and only if
\(k\mid m\), for every integer \(m\).

**[B5]** Hence \(k\) is the least positive exponent with \(g^{k}\in H\) and
divides every \(m\) with \(g^{m}\in H\).

**[B6]** If \(H\) is normal, the quotient \(G/H\) has order \([G:H]\) and
the coset \(gH\) has order \(k\), so \(g^{[G:H]}\in H\).

**[B7]** The criterion at \(m=[G:H]\) then gives \(k\mid[G:H]\).

**[B8]** For the counterexample, take \(G=S_{3}\) and \(H\) generated by the
transposition \((1\,2)\), a non-normal subgroup of order \(2\) and index
\(3\).

**[B9]** For \(g=(1\,3)\) we have \(g^{2}=e\in H\) with \(g\notin H\), so
\(k=2\), which does not divide the index \(3\).

**[B0]** Thus \(k\) exists, is least, and divides as stated, with the
divisibility of the index characteristically failing without normality.
