# b95J01: second exemplar of the InkUp style, the contrast case

Status: one worked example, written and read by model seats and reviewed by claude-12
(2026-11-24). No claim that the style is validated. Read `../a97A02/README.md` first;
this one was made the same way and records what differed.

The problem: H a subgroup of finite index in G. (a) For each g there is a least positive
k with g^k in H, and k divides every m with g^m in H. (b) If H is normal, k divides
[G:H]. (c) A counterexample to (b) without normality.

## Why this is the contrast case

In a97A02 the Lean proof and the informal solution took different routes to continuity,
and the informal one had an error; much of the teaching telling's length went on that
contrast. Here the two agree: the exponents form a subgroup T of the integers, finite
index gives a positive element, T is generated by one integer, Lagrange in G/H for the
normal case, two transpositions in S_3 for the counterexample. The only difference is
that Lean cites library lemmas for two steps (the pigeonhole step, and g^[G:H] in H)
that the informal solution proves. The teaching telling proves both; the formal-audit
telling names the lemmas.

## Questions

Three from the reader dialogue (futon6 `holes/M-reader-clear.md`), two added:

| Question | Source | Where the teaching telling answers it |
|---|---|---|
| Where is finite index used? | reader | B2: it is the only source of a positive exponent; with g of infinite order and H trivial there is none. |
| Where does the positive exponent come from? | reader | B2: pigeonhole on the cosets H, gH, ..., g^nH. |
| How does normality give k \| [G:H]? | reader | B6, B7: G/H is a group of order [G:H], gH has order k; then the criterion at m = [G:H]. |
| Why the set T, rather than a least exponent and the division algorithm? | added | B1: the same argument, packaged so that all integers, negative ones included, are handled at once. |
| Why not a 3-cycle for the counterexample? | added | B9: its least exponent is 3, which divides the index. |

The expert-reference telling answers the fourth question and no other: the plan's author
judged that the subgroup of the integers is the one idea a reference version must keep.

## What the two readers said

Both marked all ten paragraphs justified, had no questions left, and said they could
write the proof without the text. Both said the text explains why, pointing to B1, B2, B6
and B9. Both named the same two things stated without proof: that subgroups of the
integers are cyclic, and the step from equal cosets to g^(j-i) in H. Each now has a
clause in B2 and B3; that revision has not been read again.

One reader was cold. The other had asked the original questions on the print of this
problem earlier in its session, though its notes say it had not seen the problem; treat
its reading as not independent.
