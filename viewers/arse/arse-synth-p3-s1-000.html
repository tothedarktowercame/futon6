Why is the t-PushTASEP update truncated geometric?

synth-p3-s1-000  |  combinatorics probability markov-chains  |  13 nodes, 22 edges

Question

Consider the inhomogeneous multispecies $t$-PushTASEP on an $n$-site ring with rates $1/x_j$ as described in the proof of node p3-s1. When site $j$ rings and the active species $r$ sits above $m$ weaker species clockwise, the construction selects the $k$-th weaker species with probability $t^{k-1}/[m]_t$, where $[m]_t=1+t+\cdots+t^{m-1}$, and then runs the push-cascade until species $0$ is displaced. How does this truncated geometric law follow from the Macdonald Pieri rule for adding a box to $F_{\mu}(x;1,t)$, and why does it guarantee that the local update is compatible with the CTMC generator built from the modified Macdonald stationary weights?

Answer

Fix a configuration $\eta$ with active species $r$ at site $j$ and order the weaker species clockwise as $(s_1,\dots,s_m)$. Applying the one-row Macdonald Pieri rule with $q=1$ gives

$$F_{\eta^{(k)}}(x;1,t)=t^{k-1}\frac{1-t^{m-k+1}}{1-t^{m}}F_{\eta}(x;1,t),$$

where $\eta^{(k)}$ is obtained by swapping $r$ with $s_k$ and leaving the rest unchanged. The factor $t^{k-1}$ accounts for the $q=1$ Macdonald charge gained by crossing $k-1$ weaker species, while the denominator $1-t^{m}$ is the hook-length normalisation coming from the $m$ choices available. Consequently

$$\frac{F_{\eta^{(k)}}(x;1,t)}{F_{\eta}(x;1,t)}=t^{k-1}\frac{1-t}{1-t^{m}},$$

so multiplying by $(1-t^{m})/(1-t)$ yields exactly the probability mass $t^{k-1}/[m]_t$ assigned to the event that $r$ jumps past $s_k$. The sum $\sum_{k=1}^m t^{k-1}$ equals $[m]_t$, hence the truncated geometric law is normalized. Because the CTMC generator uses ratios of stationary weights, the product

$$\frac{\pi(\eta^{(k)})}{\pi(\eta)}=\frac{F_{\eta^{(k)}}^*(x;1,t)}{F_{\eta}^*(x;1,t)}=t^{k-1}\frac{1-t}{1-t^{m}}$$

matches the prescribed local probability, so the push-cascade update obeys detailed balance with the modified Macdonald stationary distribution. This derivation shows that the truncated geometric choice is not ad hoc: it is forced by the Pieri coefficients, and therefore the local rule and global stationary measure are perfectly compatible.
