How do epsilon factors force conductor matching for Rankin--Selberg integrals?

synth-p2-s5-001  |  number-theory automorphic-forms l-functions  |  13 nodes, 24 edges

Question

Fix a non-archimedean local field $F$ with additive character $\psi$ of conductor $O_F$. Let $\Pi$ (resp. $\pi$) be a generic representation of $GL_n(F)$ (resp. $GL_m(F)$), each realized in its Whittaker model. The Godement--Jacquet integral defines the conductor exponent $c_{\mathrm{GJ}}(\Pi \times \pi)=c(\Pi)+c(\pi)$, whereas the Rankin--Selberg Whittaker integral produces its own exponent through the epsilon factor $\varepsilon(s,\Pi \times \pi,\psi)=\omega(\Pi \times \pi,\psi)\,q^{-c_{\mathrm{RS}}(\Pi \times \pi)(s-\tfrac12)}$. Suppose $W_0$ and $W_{\pi}$ are the Casselman new vectors, normalized by $W_0(gk)=W_0(g)$ for $k \in K_1(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\Pi)})$, and let $u_Q$ range through the prescribed unipotent translates used in the Rankin--Selberg section.

Why must $Z_{RS}(W_0 \otimes W_{\pi},s)$ retain the same conductor as the Godement--Jacquet integral for every choice of $u_Q$, and how does the epsilon factor show that any failure of conductor matching would contradict the uniqueness of the local functional equation?

Answer

Because the Godement--Jacquet integral evaluates the matrix coefficients of the new vectors $v_0$ and $w_0$ against the characteristic function of $K_1(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\Pi)+c(\pi)})$, its conductor is by construction $c_{\mathrm{GJ}}(\Pi \times \pi)=c(\Pi)+c(\pi)$. Casselman--Shalika identifies the Whittaker new vector $W_0$ with a function supported on $N(F)T^{+}K_1(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\Pi)})$; right translation by any $u_Q$ merely translates the $N$-component and keeps the torus valuations intact, so $R(u_Q)W_0$ remains nonzero on every dominant coset that survives in the Godement--Jacquet computation. Pairing $R(u_Q)W_0$ with the new vector $W_{\pi}$ therefore produces a Rankin--Selberg integral whose Mellin transform is again supported on valuation ranges $[0,c(\Pi)+c(\pi)]$, forcing $c_{\mathrm{RS}}(\Pi \times \pi)=c(\Pi)+c(\pi)$.

The local functional equation normalizes the Rankin--Selberg side by the epsilon factor $\varepsilon(s,\Pi \times \pi,\psi)=\omega(\Pi \times \pi,\psi)\,q^{-c_{\mathrm{RS}}(\Pi \times \pi)(s-1/2)}$. If $c_{\mathrm{RS}}$ differed from $c_{\mathrm{GJ}}$, the two gamma factors could not coincide: the Godement--Jacquet functional equation has already fixed the valuation $q^{-c_{\mathrm{GJ}}(\Pi \times \pi)(s-1/2)}$. Hence any hypothetical zero of $R(u_Q)W_0$ on the relevant torus cells would force the Rankin--Selberg epsilon factor to drop in conductor and contradict the uniqueness of that functional equation. This comparison shows that the unipotent translates leave the new vector nonvanishing and that the conductors computed from the two integrals must agree.

Expressions (3)

\(c_{\mathrm{GJ}}(\Pi \times \pi)=c(\Pi)+c(\pi)\)
\(\varepsilon(s,\Pi \times \pi,\psi)=\omega(\Pi \times \pi,\psi)\,q^{-c_{\mathrm{RS}}(\Pi \times \pi)(s-\tfrac12)}\)
\(Z_{RS}(W_0\otimes W_{\pi},s)=\int_{N(F)\backslash G(F)} W_0(gu_Q)W_{\pi}(g)|\det g|^{s-\frac{n+m-1}{2}}dg\)
