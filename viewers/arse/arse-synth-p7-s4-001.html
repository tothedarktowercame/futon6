How to kill the Wall obstruction when upgrading an FH(ℚ) complex?

synth-p7-s4-001  |  algebraic-topology manifolds differential-topology  |  14 nodes, 22 edges

Question

Let $X$ be a finite CW complex whose rational homology agrees with that of a closed $n$-manifold (an $FH(\mathbb Q)$ complex in our notation), and assume $\pi_1(X)=\Gamma$ carries the 2-torsion lattice used in the proof. Suppose a degree-one normal map $f:M^n\to X$ with stable normal bundle classified by $ u$ has already been built. Wall's surgery exact sequence says that the obstruction to replacing $X$ by a closed manifold $M'$ homotopy equivalent to $X$ is the element

$$\theta(\eta(f)) \in L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb Z[\Gamma]),$$

where $\eta(f)\in[X,G/TOP]$ is the normal invariant of $f$. Which conditions on $\eta(f)$ and on the $L$-group ensure this obstruction vanishes, and how does one see that the torsion in $\Gamma$ does not reintroduce an obstruction while keeping $\pi_1(M')\cong\Gamma$?

Answer

Because $X$ is a rational Poincar\'e complex, the usual Postnikov tower plus Sullivan's rational surgery produces a degree-one normal map $f$ whose normal invariant lives in $[X,G/TOP]\cong H^0(X;\mathbb Z/2)\oplus H^2(X;\mathbb Z/2)\oplus H^4(X;\mathbb Z)\oplus\cdots$. Changing $f$ within its bordism class changes $\eta(f)$ by an arbitrary element of this cohomology group, so we may hunt for $\eta$ with trivial Wall obstruction. The relevant part of the exact sequence

$$0\to S^{TOP}(X)\to[X,G/TOP]\xrightarrow{\theta} L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb Z[\Gamma])\to0$$

pairs each normal invariant with its surgery obstruction. For the chosen 2-torsion lattice, the assembly map identifies $L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb Z[\Gamma])\otimes\mathbb Q$ with $H_n(B\Gamma;\mathbb Q)\oplus H_{n-4}(B\Gamma;\mathbb Q)\oplus\cdots$, but $B\Gamma=B K$ has only even-dimensional rational homology inherited from the $FH(\mathbb Q)$ model. The summands in degree $n$, $n-4$, ... all vanish because torsion in $\Gamma$ forces these groups to be finite, hence zero after tensoring with $\mathbb Q$. Thus $L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb Z[\Gamma])$ is torsion, generated by 2-primary classes detected by the reduced $w_2$ and $w_4$ of the normal invariant. By choosing $\eta(f)$ so that these characteristic classes match those of the $FH(\mathbb Q)$ tangent microbundle, we force $\theta(\eta(f))=0$.

With the obstruction gone, the exact sequence shows that $\eta(f)$ lifts to an element of the structure set $S^{TOP}(X)$, i.e. to a closed manifold $M'$ together with a simple homotopy equivalence $M'\to X$ inducing an isomorphism on $\pi_1$. Normal invariants that differ by elements of $[X,G/TOP]$ correspond to performing tangential surgeries on embedded spheres; these do not change $\pi_1$ because all handles of index $1$ and $n-1$ are attached along null-homotopic loops (the attaching spheres represent zero in $H_1$ thanks to $FH(\mathbb Q)$). Consequently $M'$ is a closed manifold, shares $\pi_1$ with $X$, and realizes the $FH(\mathbb Q)$ data once the $L$-group obstruction is neutralized.

Expressions (3)

\(\theta(\eta(f)) \in L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb Z[\Gamma])\)
\(0\to S^{TOP}(X)\to[X,G/TOP]\xrightarrow{\theta} L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb Z[\Gamma])\to0\)
\(L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb Z[\Gamma])\otimes\mathbb Q \cong H_n(B\Gamma;\mathbb Q)\oplus H_{n-4}(B\Gamma;\mathbb Q)\oplus\cdots\)
