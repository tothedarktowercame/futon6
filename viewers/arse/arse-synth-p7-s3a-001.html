Explicit FH(ℚ) lattices with prescribed torsion

synth-p7-s3a-001  |  algebraic-groups number-theory group-theory  |  13 nodes, 18 edges

Question

Fowler's arithmetic $FH(\mathbb Q)$ lattices are built from anisotropic quaternion algebras over number fields. If I want an explicit cocompact lattice whose torsion is exactly the cyclic group of order 5 (or order $n$ in general), how should I choose the field and order, and why does this construction still land in $FH(\mathbb Q)$ even though the resulting quotient is only an orbicomplex?

Answer

Pick a number field $F$ containing the desired cyclic group of roots of unity $\mu_n(F)$. For example, take $F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$ and let $B$ be the unique quaternion algebra over $F$ that ramifies at every real place (there are none here) and at a pair of finite places so that $B$ is a division algebra. Let $\mathcal O\subset B$ be a maximal order and set $\Gamma=\mathcal O^{1}$ (norm-one elements). The embedding $B\otimes_{F,\sigma}\mathbb R\cong \mathbf H$ gives a homomorphism $\Gamma\hookrightarrow G=\operatorname{SU}(2)^r$, whose projection to one factor yields a uniform lattice.

The torsion of $\Gamma$ consists of the units of $\mathcal O$, and these units map onto $\mu_n(F)$. By choosing $F$ with prescribed roots of unity one realizes exactly those torsion orders. The action on the associated symmetric space satisfies Fowler's Euler-vanishing hypothesis because every fixed-point set is contractible and has zero reduced Euler characteristic. Hence $\Gamma\in FH(\mathbb Q)$, producing a finite rational orbicomplex even though the torsion prevents a manifold quotient.
