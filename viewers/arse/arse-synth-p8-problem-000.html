When does a 4-face vertex admit a symplectic splitting?

synth-p8-problem-000  |  symplectic-geometry differential-topology manifolds  |  13 nodes, 20 edges

Question

In $(\mathbb R^4,\omega_0=dx_1\wedge dy_1+dx_2\wedge dy_2)$ let $K$ be a polyhedral surface whose faces are embedded Lagrangian triangles. Each vertex $p$ is incident to four faces, so the tangent cone $C_pK$ is generated by four oriented rays $v_1,\dots,v_4$ lying in Lagrangian 2-planes. Many smoothing arguments implicitly assume that $C_pK$ splits as a direct sum of two transverse Lagrangian cones, i.e. that the rays can be paired into Lagrangian bases of complementary planes. What concrete conditions on the $v_i$ ensure such a symplectic splitting exists, and why does this nondegeneracy provide a local Hamiltonian smoothing of the vertex star?

Answer

After a symplectic change of coordinates we may assume $p=0$ and $\omega_0$ has the standard matrix $J$. A 2-plane $\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^4$ is Lagrangian iff $\omega_0$ restricts to zero on it, equivalently if a spanning pair $(a,b)$ satisfies $\omega_0(a,b)=0$. For a vertex star with four edges meeting cyclically, we obtain primitive tangent vectors $v_1,\dots,v_4$ along the edges of the incident faces. The tangent cone splits as $\Lambda_+\cup\Lambda_-$ with $\Lambda_+=\operatorname{span}\{v_i,v_{i+2}\}$ and $\Lambda_- = \operatorname{span}\{v_{i+1},v_{i+3}\}$ (indices mod 4) exactly when two conditions hold:

1. **Lagrangian pairing:** $\omega_0(v_i,v_{i+2})=0=\omega_0(v_{i+1},v_{i+3})$ so that each opposite pair spans a Lagrangian plane. 2. **Nondegeneracy:** The bivectors $v_i\wedge Jv_i$ satisfy $v_1\wedge Jv_1+v_3\wedge Jv_3 = 0$ and $v_2\wedge Jv_2+v_4\wedge Jv_4 = 0$, ensuring that $\Lambda_+\oplus\Lambda_- = T_0\mathbb R^4$ as a direct sum. Geometrically this says that the projections of the four rays to the Lagrangian Grassmannian $\Lambda(2)$ land at two transverse points, so the vertex star is modeled on the union of two transverse Lagrangian half-planes.

When these conditions hold one can express the star as the union of two exact Lagrangian cones that meet cleanly along the two edges forming $\Lambda_+\cap\Lambda_-$. In Darboux coordinates the neighborhood is Hamiltonian isotopic to the union $\{y_1=0,y_2=0\}\cup\{x_1=0,x_2=0\}$. One then chooses a bump function $\rho(r)$ and solves the Cauchy–Riemann equation for a generating function $F(x_1,x_2)$ whose graph interpolates between the two cones, keeping $\partial F$ on the boundary rays to preserve the combinatorics. Because $\Lambda_+\oplus\Lambda_-=T_0\mathbb R^4$ and the rays form a symplectic basis, the resulting graph $L_F$ is the image of the vertex star under a compactly supported Hamiltonian isotopy, giving a smooth Lagrangian patch. If the balancing condition fails (e.g. $\omega_0(v_i,v_{i+2}) eq 0$) the two cones cannot be made complementary, so any attempted smoothing forces $\omega_0$ to acquire nonzero restriction, obstructing a Lagrangian smoothing. Thus the symplectic splitting criterion is precisely what guarantees the existence of a Lagrangian smoothing at a 4-face vertex.
