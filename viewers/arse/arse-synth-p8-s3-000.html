When does the vertex spanning lemma yield a symplectic direct sum?

synth-p8-s3-000  |  symplectic-geometry linear-algebra differential-geometry  |  13 nodes, 20 edges

Question

Let $K\subset(\mathbb R^4,\omega_0)$ be a polyhedral Lagrangian whose vertex star at $p$ lies in an affine hyperplane $H$. The algebraic proof of the vertex spanning lemma says that if the four incident edges span $H$ and no two Lagrangian faces coincide, then after reordering a symplectic basis $(e_1,e_3,e_2,e_4)$ we obtain a block-diagonal matrix for $\omega_0$, so $T_p\mathbb R^4=V_1\oplus V_2$ with each $V_i$ Lagrangian. What hypotheses on $H$ and on the edges are actually needed to justify this block decomposition? How does $\ker(\omega_0|_H)$ control the topology of the vertex star and guarantee that the algebraic normal form exists?

Answer

Because $K$ is Lagrangian all faces lie in 2-planes on which $\omega_0$ vanishes. If the four edges span the hyperplane $H$, then $\omega_0|_H$ is a rank-two skew form whose kernel is one-dimensional. Topologically, this kernel line is the common intersection of all Lagrangian faces: each face contains the line because its tangent plane lies inside $H$ and is isotropic for $\omega_0$. If two distinct edges were contained in the kernel, then every edge would be proportional, contradicting the assumption that the star has four distinct faces. Thus there are exactly two opposite edges spanning $\ker(\omega_0|_H)$, and the remaining two edges project to a symplectic basis of $H/\ker$.

Choose a Darboux chart with basis $(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4)$ such that $e_1$ lies along $\ker(\omega_0|_H)$ and $e_2$ projects to an edge inside $H$. Extend by $J e_1=e_3$ and $J e_2=e_4$. In this basis the matrix of $\omega_0$ takes the form

$$\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 & a & 0\\0 & 0 & 0 & b\\-a & 0 & 0 & 0\\0 & -b & 0 & 0\end{pmatrix},$$

where $a=\omega_0(e_1,e_3)$ and $b=\omega_0(e_2,e_4)$ are nonzero. The subspaces $V_1=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_3\}$ and $V_2=\operatorname{span}\{e_2,e_4\}$ are Lagrangian and complementary, so the vertex star decomposes as a direct sum of two Lagrangian fans. The algebraic block-diagonalization therefore rests on two geometric prerequisites: (1) the edges span a hyperplane $H$ transverse to $JH$ so that $\omega_0|_H$ has one-dimensional kernel, and (2) the kernel line is contained in every face but coincides with exactly one pair of opposite edges. When these hold the linear algebra argument applies and yields the symplectic direct sum used in the vertex spanning lemma.

Expressions (3)

\(\ker(\omega_0|_H) = \bigcap_{F\ni p} T_pF\)
\(\Omega = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 & a & 0\\0 & 0 & 0 & b\\-a & 0 & 0 & 0\\0 & -b & 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}\)
\(T_p\mathbb R^4 = V_1 \oplus V_2\)
