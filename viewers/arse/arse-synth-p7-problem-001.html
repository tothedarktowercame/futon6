Does torsion in a uniform lattice obstruct rationally acyclic manifolds?

synth-p7-problem-001  |  lie-groups lattice algebraic-topology manifolds  |  12 nodes, 16 edges

Question

Let $G$ be a connected semisimple Lie group with finite center and let $\Gamma<G$ be a cocompact lattice that contains a nontrivial element of order 2. If one wants a closed manifold $M$ with rationally acyclic universal cover whose fundamental group is $\Gamma$, is the presence of this torsion an actual obstruction, or can one keep the torsion and still obtain $M$ by performing equivariant surgeries on the orbifold $X/\Gamma$ where $X=G/K$ is the symmetric space?

Answer

Torsion is a hard obstruction. If $M$ is a closed manifold whose universal cover is rationally acyclic, then $\Gamma=\pi_1(M)$ acts freely on the universal cover $\widetilde M$. Every deck transformation is homotopic to the identity, so for any torsion element $g\in\Gamma$ the Lefschetz number satisfies $L(g)=1$. Lefschetz fixed-point theory therefore implies that $g$ has a fixed point in $\widetilde M$, contradicting freeness. Hence $\Gamma$ must be torsion-free.

Equivariant surgery on the orbifold $X/\Gamma$ cannot remove the fixed points without first passing to a torsion-free subgroup. Selberg's lemma provides a finite-index torsion-free subgroup $\Gamma'\subset\Gamma$, and the quotient $X/\Gamma'$ is the desired aspherical manifold with rationally acyclic cover $X$. The original lattice merely produces the orbifold $X/\Gamma$; any attempt to descend back to $\Gamma$ reintroduces isotropy and destroys the manifold structure.

Expressions (2)

\(L(g)=\sum_i (-1)^i \operatorname{tr}(g_*:H_i(\widetilde M;\mathbb Q)\to H_i(\widetilde M;\mathbb Q))=1\)
\(X=G/K,\quad M=X/\Gamma' ext{ when } \Gamma' ext{ is torsion-free}\)
