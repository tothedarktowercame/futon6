Rational Smith theory for free involutions on acyclic complexes

synth-p7-s5-001  |  algebraic-topology group-theory homology-cohomology  |  13 nodes, 20 edges

Question

Let $Y$ be a finite CW complex with a cellular involution $\tau$ and assume $H_*(Y;\mathbb Q)=0$. In the proof we let $Z/2$ act freely on such a space coming from the universal cover of a fake projective space. Textbooks on Smith theory usually state that the fixed-point set of a $p$-group action on a mod-$p$ acyclic complex is again mod-$p$ acyclic, which sounds like an obstruction to free actions. Could someone spell out why this reasoning does not apply with rational coefficients? In particular, if $\tau$ acts freely, what becomes of the Smith complex over $\mathbb Q$, and how does one see that $Y/\tau$ is still rationally acyclic while $Y^{\langle\tau\rangle}=\varnothing$?

Answer

The Smith complex is built from $C_*(Y;\mathbb F_p)$ with the maps $1\pm\tau$, and its exactness uses the equality $(1-\tau)(1+\tau)=0$ inside $\mathbb F_p[\mathbb Z/2]$. When the characteristic is $p$, the operator $1+\tau$ is nilpotent on chains, so the short exact sequence $0\to C_*(Y^{\langle\tau\rangle};\mathbb F_p)\to C_*(Y;\mathbb F_p)\xrightarrow{1+\tau} C_*(Y;\mathbb F_p)\to0$ produces the usual Smith long exact sequence and forces nonzero homology in the fixed points whenever $Y$ is mod-$p$ acyclic. Over $\mathbb Q$ the algebra changes: the group ring decomposes as $\mathbb Q[\mathbb Z/2]\cong\mathbb Q\oplus\mathbb Q$, so the averaging idempotent $e_+=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\tau)$ is invertible on the $+1$ eigenspace. The chain map $1+\tau$ now splits $C_*(Y;\mathbb Q)$ into $\pm$-eigenspaces, and the putative Smith exact sequence collapses because $e_+$ is an automorphism.

When $\tau$ acts freely, the covering projection $\pi:Y\to Y/\tau$ admits a rational transfer $tr$ defined by averaging lifts. It satisfies the identities

$$tr\circ\pi_*=2\,id_{H_*(Y;\mathbb Q)},\qquad \pi_*\circ tr=2\,id_{H_*(Y/\tau;\mathbb Q)}.$$

Since $2$ is invertible, $\pi_*$ and $tr$ are isomorphisms onto the $\pm$-eigenspaces, and $H_*(Y/\tau;\mathbb Q)\cong H_*(Y;\mathbb Q)^\tau=0$ when $Y$ is rationally acyclic. Nothing forces a fixed set because the entire argument relied on characteristic $p$. Thus a free $Z/2$-action on a rationally acyclic complex is compatible with Smith theory: the mod-$2$ version still predicts fixed points (indeed, $Y^{\langle\tau\rangle}=\varnothing$ implies $H_*(Y;\mathbb F_2) eq0$), but in characteristic zero the averaging idempotent kills the obstruction and the quotient remains rationally acyclic.
