Geometric criteria for block-diagonalizing \omega at a vertex

synth-p8-s3-001  |  symplectic-geometry linear-algebra differential-geometry  |  13 nodes, 21 edges

Question

Suppose a four-valent Lagrangian vertex of $K\subset(\mathbb R^4,\omega_0)$ sits inside an affine hyperplane $H$. The algebraic vertex spanning lemma block-diagonalizes $\omega_0$ once a basis is chosen, but it never spells out the geometric hypotheses on $H$ or on the incident faces. What are the necessary and sufficient conditions on the vertex star that ensure $H$ is coisotropic ($\dim\ker\omega_0|_H=1$) with kernel lying in each face, and how does one use this to build a symplectic basis $(e_1,e_3,e_2,e_4)$ making $\omega_0$ block diagonal?

Answer

The hyperplane condition alone is not enough: if $\omega_0|_H$ had two-dimensional kernel, then every tangent direction would be isotropic and the faces would collapse to lines. Thus we require $H$ to be coisotropic, i.e.

$$\dim H=3,\qquad \dim \ker(\omega_0|_H)=1.$$

This happens precisely when the four edges span $H$ and no three lie in a common 2-plane; under that hypothesis the restriction $\omega_0|_H$ has rank 2 with kernel generated by the unique line contained in every face. Because each face is Lagrangian the kernel line must lie inside it, and if it coincided with more than one edge every edge would be proportional. Hence we get a distinguished pair of opposite edges along $\ker(\omega_0|_H)$.

Pick coordinates so that $e_1$ points along this kernel line inside $H$. Choose $e_2$ in $H$ and transverse to the kernel, for instance along the projection of one of the remaining edges. Set $e_3=Je_1$ and $e_4=Je_2$. The matrix of $\omega_0$ in this ordered basis is

$$\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 & a & 0\\0 & 0 & 0 & b\\-a & 0 & 0 & 0\\0 & -b & 0 & 0\end{pmatrix},\quad a,b eq0,$$

which is block diagonal with blocks corresponding to $V_1=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_3\}$ and $V_2=\operatorname{span}\{e_2,e_4\}$. The kernel line sits inside both $V_1$ and every face, while the quotient $H/\ker$ inherits a genuine symplectic form that pairs the other two edges. This explicit construction shows that the topological prerequisites for the algebraic lemma are: (1) the vertex star spans a coisotropic hyperplane, (2) the unique isotropic line in $H$ is contained in exactly one pair of opposite edges, and (3) the remaining edges project to a basis of $H/\ker$. When these hold, $\omega_0$ admits the desired block form and $\mathbb R^4$ splits as $V_1\oplus V_2$ with each $V_i$ Lagrangian.

Expressions (3)

\(\dim\ker(\omega_0|_H)=1\)
\(\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 & a & 0\\0 & 0 & 0 & b\\-a & 0 & 0 & 0\\0 & -b & 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}\)
\(\mathbb R^4 = V_1 \oplus V_2,\ V_i\text{ Lagrangian}\)
