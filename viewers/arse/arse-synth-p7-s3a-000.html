Torsion orders in Fowler's arithmetic FH(ℚ) lattices

synth-p7-s3a-000  |  algebraic-groups number-theory group-theory  |  13 nodes, 20 edges

Question

Fowler's Section 5 constructs arithmetic lattices in semisimple groups and shows they lie in $FH(\mathbb Q)$. Which torsion orders can actually occur in these examples, and how do the number-theoretic choices (quadratic fields, quaternion algebras, etc.) control the finite subgroups that appear in the resulting lattices?

Answer

Start with an absolutely almost simple $\mathbb Q$-group $\mathbf G$ that is anisotropic over $\mathbb Q$ at infinity (for instance, the norm-one group of a quaternion algebra that ramifies at every real place). Let $F/\mathbb Q$ be a number field and choose an order $\mathcal O\subset F$. The arithmetic lattice $\Gamma=\mathbf G(\mathcal O)$ embeds cocompactly in the real Lie group $G=\mathbf G(\mathbb R)$. Finite subgroups of $\Gamma$ are precisely the torsion units of $\mathbf G(\mathcal O)$, so their orders divide the orders of elements in $\mathbf G(\mathcal O_F)$, where $F$ is the splitting field of $\mathbf G$.

For quaternion-algebra examples the torsion comes from the roots of unity in $F$. Choosing $F=\mathbb Q(i)$ or $\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$ produces lattices with 4- or 6-torsion; taking $F$ with class number one and no nontrivial roots of unity yields torsion-free lattices. Thus Fowler's arithmetic constructions realize every torsion order that already occurs among the units of the chosen number field. Each such lattice still lies in $FH(\mathbb Q)$ because the associated orbicomplex satisfies the fixed-set Euler-vanishing hypothesis, but only the torsion-free specializations give genuine manifold quotients.

Expressions (3)

\(\Gamma=\mathbf G(\mathcal O) \subset \mathbf G(\mathbb R)\)
\(\text{torsion}(\Gamma)=\mu(F)\)
\(F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_n) \Rightarrow n ext{-torsion in }\Gamma\)
