Hamiltonian interpolation along an edge arc

synth-p8-s5-001  |  symplectic-geometry differential-topology surgery-theory  |  13 nodes, 18 edges

Question

Two product neighborhoods around consecutive vertices of a polyhedral Lagrangian $K\subset(\mathbb R^4,\omega_0)$ overlap along an edge arc $\gamma$. Inside each neighborhood the Lagrangian is the graph of an exact 1-form $dS_i$ over a common 2-disk. Lees/Weinstein give local coordinates in the smooth case, but here $\gamma$ sits in the piecewise-linear locus. What conditions on $\gamma$ and on the primitives $S_1,S_2$ guarantee that the interpolated function $S_\varepsilon(x)=\chi(x_1/\varepsilon)S_1+(1-\chi)S_2$ still produces a Hamiltonian smoothing that matches the vertex models at the ends of $\gamma$?

Answer

Assume first that the two vertex neighborhoods have already been smoothed so that near the endpoints of $\gamma$ the Lagrangian is smooth and coincides with the graphs of $dS_1$ and $dS_2$. The edge arc is then contained in the intersection of two Darboux charts in which $\gamma$ is straight and the sheets are exact graphs. The only obstruction to applying the standard Hamiltonian interpolation is the piecewise-linear transition along $\gamma$; to rule it out we require:

1. **Exactness along the edge:** the forms $\lambda_{\omega_0}|_{L_i}$ admit primitives $S_i$ that extend continuously across $\gamma$. This is automatic for polyhedral Lagrangians built from exact faces. 2. **$C^1$ matching on the edge:** $S_1$ and $S_2$ agree together with their first derivatives along $x_2=0$ (the line representing $\gamma$) so that their graphs share the same tangent plane. This condition ensures the piecewise-linear crease is only second order. 3. **Compact support of the difference:** $S_1-S_2$ vanishes near the endpoints of $\gamma$ so that the interpolation leaves the vertex neighborhoods untouched.

Under these hypotheses the cutoff construction

$$S_\varepsilon(x)=\chi\Big(\frac{x_1}{\varepsilon}\Big) S_1(x)+\left(1-\chi\Big(\frac{x_1}{\varepsilon}\Big)\right) S_2(x)$$

defines a $C^\infty$ function equal to $S_1$ for $x_1\gg0$ and to $S_2$ for $x_1\ll0$. Its graph is exact Lagrangian because $\chi$ depends only on $x_1$, so $dS_\varepsilon$ is a convex combination of $dS_1$ and $dS_2$ plus a correction supported where $\chi' eq0$. The family $S_t$ ($t\in[0,1]$) connecting $S_1$ to $S_\varepsilon$ yields a compactly supported Hamiltonian isotopy supported in the overlap of the vertex neighborhoods. The resulting smoothing matches the vertex data at both ends of $\gamma$ and replaces the polyhedral crease by a smooth strip. Thus the Lees/Weinstein surgery extends to the PL setting whenever the edge arc admits exact primitives that agree to first order along $\gamma$.

Expressions (3)

\(S_\varepsilon(x)=\chi\Big(\frac{x_1}{\varepsilon}\Big) S_1(x)+(1-\chi\Big(\frac{x_1}{\varepsilon}\Big)) S_2(x)\)
\(H_t(x) = -\partial_t S_t(x)\)
\(L_t = \operatorname{graph}(dS_t)\)
