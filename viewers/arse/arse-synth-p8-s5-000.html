Extending Hamiltonian edge smoothing to polyhedral Lagrangians

synth-p8-s5-000  |  symplectic-geometry differential-topology surgery-theory  |  13 nodes, 18 edges

Question

Lees' and Weinstein's neighborhood theorems are stated for smooth Lagrangian embeddings. Suppose instead we have a polyhedral Lagrangian $K\subset(\mathbb R^4,\omega_0)$ whose edge $e$ is the intersection of two smooth Lagrangian sheets $L_1$ and $L_2$ meeting along $e$ with a common tangent line but distinct second-order data (a crease). Under what hypotheses can we still model a neighborhood of $e$ by exact Lagrangian graphs and perform the usual Hamiltonian interpolation $S_\varepsilon=\chi(x_1/\varepsilon)S_1+(1-\chi)S_2$ to obtain a smooth edge?

Answer

The key observation is that the hypotheses of the Lagrangian neighborhood theorem are local and only require a smooth structure along the edge. If each face of the polyhedral Lagrangian is $C^1$ up to the edge and the two faces share the same tangent line along $e$, then $e$ admits a tubular neighborhood in which $K$ is the union of two exact Lagrangian graphs. Concretely, pick Darboux coordinates $(x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2)$ with $e=\{x_2=y_1=y_2=0\}$ and such that each sheet is the graph of $dS_i(x_1,x_2)$ for $i=1,2$. The generating functions satisfy $S_1=S_2$ along $x_2=0$ and have matching first derivatives because both sheets agree along $e$; the difference lies in the second fundamental form (the crease).

Under these conditions we can interpolate by the usual cutoff construction

$$S_\varepsilon(x_1,x_2)=\chi\Big(\frac{x_1}{\varepsilon}\Big) S_1(x_1,x_2)+\big(1-\chi\Big(\frac{x_1}{\varepsilon}\Big)\big) S_2(x_1,x_2),$$

where $\chi$ is a smooth bump function equal to 1 near $x_1>0$ and 0 near $x_1<0$. The equality of $S_1$ and $S_2$ (and their first derivatives) along $x_2=0$ ensures that $S_\varepsilon$ is $C^1$ and hence defines an exact Lagrangian graph whose boundary matches the unsmoothed vertex neighborhoods. Because the construction is exact, the resulting graph differs from the original union by a compactly supported Hamiltonian isotopy; the Hamiltonian is a primitive of $\partial_t S_t$ in the usual generating-function proof.

Thus the local surgery theorem extends to polyhedral Lagrangians provided: (1) each face is smooth up to the edge, (2) the tangent data agree along the edge (so the crease is only second-order), and (3) the faces are exact in a neighborhood of the edge. These assumptions guarantee that Lees'/Weinstein's coordinates still exist and that the bump-function interpolation produces a smooth Lagrangian matching the polyhedral configuration outside the chosen neighborhood.

Expressions (3)

\(L_i = \operatorname{graph}(dS_i)\)
\(S_\varepsilon(x) = \chi\Big(\frac{x_1}{\varepsilon}\Big) S_1(x) + \big(1-\chi\Big(\frac{x_1}{\varepsilon}\Big)\big) S_2(x)\)
\(L_{\varepsilon} = \operatorname{graph}(dS_\varepsilon)\)
