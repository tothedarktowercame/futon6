When do Rankin–Selberg and Godement–Jacquet conductors coincide?

synth-p7-s4-000  |  number-theory automorphic-forms l-functions  |  13 nodes, 22 edges

Question

Let $F$ be a non-archimedean local field with additive character $\psi$ of conductor $\mathfrak o_F$, and let $\Pi$ be an irreducible generic representation of $GL_n(F)$ with Whittaker model $\mathcal W(\Pi,\psi)$. Denote by $W_0$ the Casselman new vector normalized by $W_0(1)=1$. For any generic representation $\pi$ of $GL_m(F)$ with Whittaker new vector $W'_0$ consider the translated Rankin–Selberg integral

$$Z(s,W_0,W'_0;u_Q)=\int_{N_m\\backslash GL_m(F)} W_0(j(g)u_Q) W'_0(g) |\det g|^{s-(n-1)/2}\,dg.$$

Expositions often claim that one can vary only the unipotent element $u_Q$ to match the conductor of $\pi$, and that this forces the Rankin–Selberg conductor to agree with the Godement–Jacquet conductor exponent $f(\Pi\times\pi)$. What hypotheses ensure this equality, and why is $W_0$ a universal choice once $u_Q$ is allowed to depend on $\pi$?

Answer

Fix $\psi$ with conductor $\mathfrak o_F$ and let $W_0$ be the Casselman–Shalika new vector, so that $W_0$ is right invariant under $K_1(\mathfrak p^{c(\Pi)})$ and its support lies in $N T^{+} K_1(\mathfrak p^{c(\Pi)})$. The Casselman–Shalika formula shows that on a diagonal element $a=\operatorname{diag}(\varpi^{\lambda_1},\dots,\varpi^{\lambda_n})$ with $\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_n\geq 0$, the value $W_0(a)$ equals $\Delta(a)^{1/2}$ times the Satake polynomial of $\Pi$ evaluated at $q^{-\lambda_i}$. Consequently $Z(s,W_0,W'_0;u_Q)$ vanishes unless the lower-left block of $j(g)u_Q$ carries at least $c(\pi)$ powers of $\varpi$; this is the same congruence restriction defining new vectors for $GL_m$.

Choose $u_Q$ in the mirabolic subgroup whose reduction is the identity modulo $\mathfrak p^{c(\Pi)}$ except for a single row that encodes the valuations demanded by $Q$. Because $u_Q\in K_1(\mathfrak p^{c(\Pi)}) N$, the translate $R(u_Q)W_0$ still lies in the one-dimensional space of $K_1(\mathfrak p^{c(\Pi)})$-fixed Whittaker functions, so it equals $W_0$ up to a nonzero scalar and remains a test vector. For any generic $\pi$ with conductor $c(\pi)$, pick a JPSS new vector $W'_0$ fixed by $K_1(\mathfrak p^{c(\pi)})$. The local functional equation of Jacquet–Piatetski-Shapiro–Shalika then yields

$$\varepsilon(s,\Pi\times\pi,\psi)=q^{-(c(\Pi)+c(\pi))(s-1/2)}\cdot\gamma_0,$$

where $\gamma_0$ is a unit independent of $s$. Comparing both sides shows that $f_{RS}(\Pi\times\pi)=c(\Pi)+c(\pi)$: the $u_Q$-translate enforces exactly the conductor predicted by Godement–Jacquet because the matrix coefficients entering the integral vanish for smaller exponents, while scaling by $\varpi^{c(\pi)}$ introduces the required power of $q^{-s}$. Hence a single $W_0$ suffices; all conductor variation is absorbed by $u_Q$ and $W'_0$, so the Rankin–Selberg integral matches the Godement–Jacquet conductor and the $\varepsilon$-factor constraints of JPSS (1981, §5).
