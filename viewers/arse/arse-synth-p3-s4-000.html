Why is the q=1 AMW ring chain irreducible with positive normalization?

synth-p3-s4-000  |  probability markov-chains combinatorics  |  13 nodes, 24 edges

Question

The AMW $q=1$ theorem asserts that on the ring sector $S_n(\lambda)$ with $x_i>0$ and $0\le t<1$ the inhomogeneous multispecies exclusion chain has stationary law $\pi(\eta)=F_{\eta}(x;1,t)/P_{\lambda}(x;1,t)$, where $P_{\lambda}(x;1,t)=\sum_{ u\in S_n(\lambda)}F_{ u}(x;1,t)$. However, the proof sketch in node p3-s4 assumes the process is irreducible on that sector and that the normalization sum is strictly positive without giving details. How can we check irreducibility of the $q=1$ ring chain and verify that the sector sum $P_{\lambda}(x;1,t)$ is finite and positive, thereby justifying the use of the AMW stationary distribution?

Answer

The state space $S_n(\lambda)$ consists of permutations of the multiset with content $\lambda$. Every generator of the ring process swaps two adjacent species whenever the active clock rings and the weaker particle lies ahead, so any elementary transposition that respects the content occurs at positive rate. Because adjacent transpositions generate the symmetric group, every configuration can be reached from any other through a sequence of such swaps, proving irreducibility on the fixed-content sector.

For positivity, recall that the modified Macdonald polynomials are symmetric power series with nonnegative coefficients when $x_i>0$ and $0\le t<1$; in particular $F_{\eta}(x;1,t)>0$ for every configuration $\eta$. The sector sum

$\qquad P_{\lambda}(x;1,t)=\sum_{ u\in S_n(\lambda)}F_{ u}(x;1,t)$

is finite because the sector contains only $|S_n(\lambda)|$ states, and it is strictly positive because each summand is positive. Consequently the stationary weights $\pi(\eta)=F_{\eta}(x;1,t)/P_{\lambda}(x;1,t)$ form a bona fide probability measure, and the irreducibility argument implies that this measure is the unique stationary distribution described by the AMW theorem.

Expressions (3)

\(\pi(\eta)=\frac{F_{\eta}(x;1,t)}{P_{\lambda}(x;1,t)}\)
\(P_{\lambda}(x;1,t)=\sum_{\nu\in S_n(\lambda)}F_{\nu}(x;1,t)\)
\(F_{\eta}(x;1,t)>0 ext{ for } x_i>0, 0\le t<1\)
