Patching local Hamiltonian isotopies into a global smoothing

synth-p8-s6-000  |  symplectic-geometry differential-topology manifolds  |  13 nodes, 19 edges

Question

After smoothing each vertex and edge of a polyhedral Lagrangian via local Hamiltonian isotopies supported in balls $B_i$ and tubular neighborhoods $N_j$, how can we be sure these local moves glue to a global smoothing of $K\subset(\mathbb R^4,\omega_0)$? Under what conditions on the supports and on the primitives do the local Hamiltonian flows commute, and how does McDuff–Salamon's composition lemma guarantee that the combined isotopy remains compactly supported and converges to the original $K$ in $C^0$?

Answer

Let $\{B_i\}$ be disjoint Darboux balls around the vertices and $\{N_j\}$ disjoint tubular neighborhoods of the edge segments between them. By construction the vertex and edge isotopies are exact and supported inside these sets, so their Hamiltonians $H_i(t,\cdot)$ and $G_j(t,\cdot)$ vanish outside $B_i$ and $N_j$ respectively. Because the supports are disjoint, the associated flows commute (McDuff–Salamon Prop. 3.17): if $\phi_t^i$ and $\psi_t^j$ are the local isotopies, then $\phi_t^i\psi_t^j=\psi_t^j\phi_t^i$, and the product flow is generated by the Hamiltonian sum

$$H_t(x)=\sum_i H_i(t,x)+\sum_j G_j(t,x).$$

We also require that the local surgeries match along the boundaries of the neighborhoods. This is achieved by choosing the vertex smoothing so that $\partial B_i\cap K$ is a flat annulus, and by arranging the edge interpolations to agree with the vertex models on $\partial N_j$. Consequently the total isotopy is the identity on $\partial B_i$ and $\partial N_j$, so the patched map is smooth across the overlaps.

With these hypotheses the global isotopy $\Phi_t$, obtained by composing the finitely many commuting local flows, is compactly supported in $\bigcup_i B_i\cup\bigcup_j N_j$. Moreover, $\Phi_t(K)$ converges to $K$ in the $C^0$ topology as $t\to0$ because each local isotopy does, and outside the supports $\Phi_t$ is the identity. This recovers a smooth Lagrangian $K_1$ that coincides with the original polyhedral $K$ outside the chosen neighborhoods and differs from $K$ by a Hamiltonian isotopy, completing the global patching step.

Expressions (3)

\(H_t = \sum_i H_i(t,x) + \sum_j G_j(t,x)\)
\(\Phi_t = \prod_i \phi_t^i \prod_j \psi_t^j\)
\(\Phi_t(K) \to K \text{ in } C^0\)
