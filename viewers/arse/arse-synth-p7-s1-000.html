Why X/Γ is an orbifold when Γ has torsion

synth-p7-s1-000  |  lie-groups orbifolds differential-topology  |  13 nodes, 20 edges

Question

Let $X=G/K$ be the contractible symmetric space of a connected semisimple Lie group with finite center and let $\Gamma< G$ be a uniform lattice that contains an involution. Why does the quotient $X/\Gamma$ fail to be a manifold, and how can one describe the local orbifold chart near the image of a fixed point of that involution?

Answer

If $\Gamma$ is torsion-free then its action on $X$ is free and properly discontinuous, so the quotient $X/\Gamma$ is a manifold and in fact aspherical. When $\Gamma$ contains torsion, pick $x\in X$ fixed by an element $\gamma\in\Gamma$ of finite order. The stabilizer $\Gamma_x$ is finite (because $G$ acts properly on $X$), and the linear isotropy representation identifies $\Gamma_x$ with a finite subgroup of $O(T_xX)$. Thus the action fails to be free precisely on the fixed-point set $X^{\gamma}$, and the quotient is modelled on the linear slice $T_xX/\Gamma_x$ rather than $T_xX$.

The slice theorem provides a $\Gamma_x$-invariant neighborhood $U$ of $x$ such that the $\Gamma$-saturation $\Gamma\cdot U$ projects homeomorphically onto $(U/\Gamma_x)\subset X/\Gamma$. Because $\Gamma_x$ is finite, $U/\Gamma_x$ is a classical orbifold chart. Therefore $X/\Gamma$ is an orbifold whose singular locus corresponds to the images of fixed-point sets of torsion elements, and only after passing to a torsion-free finite-index subgroup does one recover a genuine manifold quotient.
