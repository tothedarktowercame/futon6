Applying Fowler's FH(Q) criterion to torsion lattices

synth-p7-s3-000  |  algebraic-topology homotopy-theory group-theory  |  13 nodes, 17 edges

Question

Fowler's $FH(\mathbb Q)$ condition asks for a proper cocompact $\Gamma$-complex whose isotropy lies in a family $\mathcal F$ and whose fixed-point Euler characteristics vanish. Let $\Gamma$ be a torsion lattice acting on $X=G/K$ with finite stabilizers. Assuming that every finite subgroup $H\subset\Gamma$ has $\chi(X^H)=0$ for $H e\{1\}$, why does Fowler's theorem place $\Gamma$ in $FH(\mathbb Q)$?

Answer

The $FH(\mathbb Q)$ property means that $\Gamma$ admits a finite $\Gamma$-CW complex $Y$ such that every cell stabilizer is finite and whose Bredon homology with coefficients in $\mathbb Q$ is finitely generated in each degree. Fowler constructs $Y$ from $X$ by attaching finitely many equivariant cells to kill the compactly supported homology of each fixed-point set $X^H$. The hypotheses that $X$ is contractible, the action is proper cocompact, and $\chi(X^H)=0$ for nontrivial $H$ imply that all obstruction groups vanish, so the cellular chain complex $C_*^{\mathcal F}(Y;\mathbb Q)$ becomes finite.

Concretely, the spectral sequence with $E^1_{p,q}=\bigoplus_{[\sigma]} H_q(\Gamma_{\sigma};\mathbb Q)$ collapses because $\Gamma_{\sigma}$ is finite and the Euler vanishing forces acyclicity above degree 0. The resulting Bredon chain complex for $Y$ provides a finite projective resolution of the constant functor $\underline{\mathbb Q}$, so $\Gamma\in FH(\mathbb Q)$. This is the equivariant finiteness conclusion used to show that torsion lattices admit finite rational orbicomplex models even though they need not be fundamental groups of manifolds.

Expressions (3)

\(E^1_{p,q}=\bigoplus_{[\sigma]} H_q(\Gamma_{\sigma};\mathbb Q)\)
\(\chi(X^H)=0 ext{ for all }1\ne H\subset\Gamma\)
\(C_*^{\mathcal F}(Y;\mathbb Q) ext{ is finite projective}\)
