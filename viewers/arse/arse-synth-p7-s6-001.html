Composing finiteness and surgery for rational PD groups

synth-p7-s6-001  |  algebraic-topology manifolds homotopy-theory  |  13 nodes, 20 edges

Question

Rational Poincaré duality groups are often said to behave like fundamental groups of closed manifolds. Suppose $\Gamma$ is such a group in dimension $n$, $\omega(\Gamma)\in\widetilde{K}_0(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])$ vanishes, and the 2-torsion lattice hypotheses guarantee the relevant $L$-group assembly map is zero. How do these ingredients combine to produce an actual closed $n$-manifold with $\pi_1=\Gamma$? In particular, how do we pass from the algebraic PD condition to an $\mathrm{FH}(\mathbb{Q})$ complex, then to a degree-one normal map, and finally through Wall's surgery exact sequence to eliminate the obstruction class?

Answer

Start with the finiteness obstruction. A finitely presented $\mathrm{PD}_n^{\mathbb{Q}}$ group $\Gamma$ has a classifying space $B\Gamma$ whose universal cover is $\mathbb{Q}$-acyclic. Wall's obstruction $\omega(\Gamma)\in\widetilde{K}_0(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])$ measures whether this $\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]$-complex is chain-homotopy equivalent to a finite complex. The assumption $\omega(\Gamma)=0$ lets us replace $B\Gamma$ by a finite CW complex $X$ with $\pi_1(X)=\Gamma$. Because $\Gamma$ is $\mathrm{PD}_n^{\mathbb{Q}}$, cap product with the fundamental class in $H_n(X;\mathbb{Q})$ furnishes isomorphisms $H^i(X;\mathbb{Q})\cong H_{n-i}(X;\mathbb{Q})$, so $X$ is a rational Poincaré ($\mathrm{FH}(\mathbb{Q})$) complex.

Next we build a geometric representative. Take the $n$-skeleton of $X$, attach handles to obtain an $n$-manifold with boundary, and double it to obtain an $n$-manifold $M$ together with a degree-one map $f:M\to X$. Stabilizing the normal bundle gives a well-defined normal invariant $\eta(f)\in[X,G/\mathrm{TOP}]$. The Wall surgery machine packages the remaining obstruction into

$$\theta(\eta(f))\in L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]),$$

computed via the exact sequence $0\to S^{\mathrm{TOP}}(X)\to[X,G/\mathrm{TOP}]\xrightarrow{\theta}L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])$. The 2-torsion lattice hypotheses ensure that the assembly map $H_n(B\Gamma;\mathbb{L}_0)\to L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])$ is trivial, so the obstruction lives in a torsion subgroup generated by low-degree characteristic classes. By adjusting $\eta(f)$ within $[X,G/\mathrm{TOP}]$ (changing the normal data on finitely many cells) we can arrange $\theta(\eta(f))=0$.

Once the obstruction vanishes, $\eta(f)$ lifts to the structure set $S^{\mathrm{TOP}}(X)$. This lift corresponds to a closed manifold $N$ and a simple homotopy equivalence $N\to X$ inducing $\pi_1(N)\cong\Gamma$. Thus the chain of implications is: $\omega(\Gamma)=0$ $\Rightarrow$ finite $\mathrm{FH}(\mathbb{Q})$ complex $\Rightarrow$ degree-one normal map $\Rightarrow$ surgery obstruction in $L_n^{\langle1\rangle}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])$ $\Rightarrow$ (after vanishing) a genuine closed manifold with fundamental group $\Gamma$.

In short, finiteness gives a space of the right homotopy type, rational PD ensures it satisfies the Poincaré duality hypotheses of surgery theory, and the vanishing of the $L$-group obstruction completes the passage to the manifold category.
