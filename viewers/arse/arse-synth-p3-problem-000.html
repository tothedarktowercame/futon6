When do starred modified Macdonald polynomials equal the unstarred ones?

synth-p3-problem-000  |  combinatorics probability symmetric-functions  |  13 nodes, 22 edges

Question

Let $\lambda$ be a partition with $\lambda_n=0$ and let $S_n(\lambda)$ be the content-fixed sector appearing in the multispecies CTMC construction for modified Macdonald polynomials. For $x_i>0$ and $0\le t<1$ the proposed stationary law is $\pi(\mu)=F_{\mu}^*(x;1,t)/P_{\lambda}^*(x;1,t)$, where $P_{\lambda}^*(x;1,t)=\sum_{ u\in S_n(\lambda)}F_{ u}^*(x;1,t)$. Most references assert without proof that the starred specialization differs from the unstarred modified Macdonald $F_{\mu}(x;1,t)$ only by an explicit product in the parts of $\mu$. Could someone explain precisely when $F_{\mu}^*=F_{\mu} \cdot c_{\mu}(t)$ and how that ratio guarantees that the CTMC stationary weights patch from the local (single site) normalization to the global sector sum?

Answer

The starred normalization is the plethystic evaluation $F_{\mu}^*(x;q,t)=F_{\mu}[X/(1-t);q,t]$ multiplied by the standard Macdonald scalar $b_{\mu}(q,t)$. In the $q=1$ specialisation used for the CTMC we have

$$F_{\mu}^*(x;1,t)=\frac{F_{\mu}(x;1,t)}{\prod_{i\ge 1}(t;t)_{m_i(\mu)}},\qquad (t;t)_m=\prod_{j=1}^m(1-t^j).$$

The factor depends only on the multiplicities $m_i(\mu)$ of each part size, so whenever $x_i>0$ and $0\le t<1$ the ratio is finite and positive. Consequently the stationary law

$$\pi(\mu)=\frac{F_{\mu}^*(x;1,t)}{P_{\lambda}^*(x;1,t)}=\frac{F_{\mu}(x;1,t)}{\displaystyle \sum_{ u\in S_n(\lambda)} F_{ u}(x;1,t) \prod_{i\ge 1}(t;t)_{m_i( u)}^{-1}}\prod_{i\ge 1}(t;t)_{m_i(\mu)}^{-1}$$

is just the unstarred weight multiplied by the local product coming from plethystic substitution. The local-to-global compatibility follows because the CTMC transition rates are expressed in terms of ratios $F_{\mu}^*/F_{ u}^*$, and the common factors $(t;t)_{m_i}$ cancel except for the multiplicities that actually change, matching the combinatorial updates of the clock process. Thus the star vs non-star bridge is completely explicit: the starred law agrees with the unstarred one after dividing by the hook-content-type product, so the global sector sum remains a true probability normalization.

Expressions (3)
