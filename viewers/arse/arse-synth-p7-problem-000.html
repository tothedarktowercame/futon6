Can a torsion lattice be \pi_1 of a manifold with rationally acyclic cover?

synth-p7-problem-000  |  lie-groups lattice algebraic-topology manifolds  |  13 nodes, 18 edges

Question

Let $G$ be a connected semisimple Lie group with finite center and let $\Gamma< G$ be a cocompact lattice containing a nontrivial element $g$ of order 2. Could such a $\Gamma$ be realized as $\pi_1(M)$ for a closed manifold $M$ whose universal cover $\widetilde M$ is rationally acyclic (i.e. $H_i(\widetilde M;\mathbb Q)=0$ for $i>0$)? I know that torsion-free compact lattices give closed aspherical manifolds $G/K/\Gamma$, but I am unsure whether the presence of 2-torsion merely produces orbifold singularities or whether it outright obstructs any manifold model with rationally acyclic cover.

Answer

No. If $M$ is a closed manifold and $\widetilde M$ is its universal cover, then the deck transformation group is isomorphic to $\pi_1(M)$ and acts freely and properly discontinuously on $\widetilde M$. Freeness forces every nontrivial element of $\Gamma$ to move every point of $\widetilde M$, so $\Gamma$ must be torsion-free; an element $g$ of order 2 would fix some point of $\widetilde M$ by the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem (because the Lefschetz number of an orientation-preserving involution on a rationally acyclic compact manifold is 1), contradicting freeness.

Therefore a uniform lattice with 2-torsion cannot arise as the fundamental group of any closed manifold whose universal cover is rationally acyclic. The best one can obtain from such a lattice is a compact orbifold $\Gamma\backslash X$, where $X=G/K$ is the symmetric space. To produce a genuine manifold, one must pass to a torsion-free finite-index subgroup of $\Gamma$; Selberg's lemma guarantees the existence of such a subgroup, and its quotient yields the desired manifold while the original lattice only gives the orbifold.

Expressions (3)

\(H_i(\widetilde M;\mathbb Q)=0 ext{ for } i>0\)
\(L(g)=\sum_i (-1)^i \operatorname{tr}(g_*:H_i(\widetilde M;\mathbb Q)\to H_i(\widetilde M;\mathbb Q)) =1\)
\(X/\Gamma ext{ is an orbifold when } \Gamma ext{ has torsion}\)
