409\title{Brown representability does not come for free}
410\begin{abstract}
411We exhibit a triangulated category $\ct$ having both products and
412coproducts and a triangulated subcategory $\cs\subset \ct$ which
413is both localizing and colocalizing, and for which
414neither a Bousfield localization nor a colocalization exists.
415It follows that neither the category $\cs$ nor its dual satisfy Brown representability.
416Our example involves an abelian category whose derived category
417does not have small Hom-sets.
418\end{abstract}
419\keywords{Brown representability, triangulated category, Bousfield localization}
420\maketitle
421\section*{Introduction}
422\label{S0}
423In recent years, several authors have proved remarkable generalizations
424of Brown's representability theorem~\cite{Brown62}; see, for example,
425\cite{Franke97,Krause02,Neeman96A,Neeman99}. It therefore becomes important
426to have an example of a triangulated category where Brown
427representability fails. In~this short note we produce such a category.
428There has also been considerable activity on the subject of
429localization in homotopy theory, and in particular on Bousfield's problem of proving
430the existence of localization of spaces with respect to
431cohomology theories.
432In \cite{Casacuberta-Scevenels-Smith05} it was shown that the existence of cohomological localizations
433follows from
434a suitable large-cardinal axiom, although Bousfield's problem remains open under the ZFC axioms alone.
435In a similar vein, it was asked in
436\cite[p.\ 35]{Hovey-Palmieri-Strickland} if every localizing subcategory
437(i.e., one which is closed under triangles and coproducts) of a stable homotopy category
438admits a Bousfield localization. Although the answer is not known in ZFC either,
439the counterexample displayed in the present article shows that Bousfield localizations
440need not exist for localizing subcategories of arbitrary triangulated categories.
441More explicitly, we show that there is an abelian category $\ca$, due to Freyd, for which
442the following holds:
443\be
444\item
445The category $\ca$ satisfies the [AB5] and [AB$4^*$] conditions (it has exact products
446and coproducts, and filtered colimits are exact).
447\item
448Nevertheless, the derived category $\D(\ca)$ does not
449have small $\Hom$-sets. That is, there is a proper class of morphisms between
450certain objects of~$\D(\ca)$.
451\item
452Let $\K(\ca)$ be the homotopy category of chain complexes
453in $\ca$, and let $\Ac(\ca)$ be the full subcategory of acyclic
454complexes. Then $\Ac(\ca)$ is both a localizing and a
455colocalizing subcategory, but neither a Bousfield localization
456nor a colocalization exist for $\Ac(\ca)$ in $\K(\ca)$.
457\item
458Neither the category $\Ac(\ca)$ nor its dual satisfy Brown representability.
459\ee
460\section{Description and proof}
461\label{S1}
462In his 1964 book \cite[Chapter~6, Exercise~A, pp.\ 131--132]{Freyd66},
463Freyd constructed an interesting abelian category. We briefly paraphrase
464the construction. In this article, our foundational formalism for categories is that of
465Mac Lane \cite[I.6]{MacLane71}.
466Let $I$ be the class of all small ordinals, and let $R=\zz[I]$ be the
467polynomial ring freely generated by $I$. The ring $R$ has a proper
468class of elements, but for what we will do this is no problem.
469Let $\ca$ be the abelian category of all small $R$-modules.
470Thus
471an object in $\ca$ is a small abelian group $M$ together with endomorphisms $\ph_i\colon M\la M$ for every $i\in I$
472, such that all the $\ph_i$ commute.
473The morphisms in $\ca$ are the $R$-module homomorphisms. Given
474two objects $M$ and $N$ in~$\ca$, there is only a small set of
475morphisms $\Hom_\ca(M,N)$; it is a subset of the set of abelian group homomorphisms.
476Note that the abelian category $\ca$ has many
477good properties. It satisfies the [AB5] and [AB$4^*$] conditions.
478After all, it is the category of modules over a ring, albeit a
479very large ring. However, there is no generator or cogenerator, and
480it will follow from our remarks that there are not enough projectives or injectives.
481Let $\zz\in\ca$ be the trivial $R$-module.
482Thus
483the underlying abelian group is the additive group of integers $\zz$, and
484all the maps $\ph_i\colon\zz\la\zz$ are zero.
485The following observation is due to Freyd \cite{Freyd66}.
486\lem{L1.3}
487With the notation as above, $\Ext^1_\ca(\zz,\zz)$ is a proper class.
488\elem
489\prf
490For every ordinal $i\in I$ we construct a module $M_i$ such that, as an abelian
491group, $M_i=\zz\oplus\zz$. The endomorphisms $\ph_j\colon M_i\la M_i$ are given by
492the following rule:
493\be
494\item
495If $j\neq i$, then $\ph_j\colon M_i\la M_i$ is zero.
496\item
497The map $\ph_i\colon M_i\la M_i$ is determined by the matrix
498\[
499\left(
500\begin{array}{cc}
5010 & 1\\
5020 & 0
503\end{array}
504\right).
505\] \ee It is clear that the $M_i$ are pairwise non-isomorphic as $R$-modules, since the element $j\in I$ for which $\ph_j$ is nonzero on $M_i$ changes as we change~$i$.
506Hence,
507we have a proper class of non-isomorphic modules $M_i$, each of which fits in an exact sequence \[ \CD 0 @>>> \zz @>>> M_i @>>> \zz @>>> 0, \endCD \] and we have produced a proper class of elements in $\Ext^1_\ca(\zz,\zz)$.
508\eprf
509Now consider the category $\K(\ca)$, the homotopy category of~$\ca$.
510The objects are chain complexes of small $R$-modules, and
511the morphisms are homotopy equivalence classes of chain maps.
512Each $R$-module is viewed as a chain complex concentrated in degree zero.
513Let $\Ac(\ca)\subset\K(\ca)$ be the full subcategory of all acyclic
514complexes. Both $\K(\ca)$ and
515$\Ac(\ca)$ are triangulated categories with small $\Hom$-sets.
516In what follows, we refer to \cite{Neeman99} for the necessary terminology and basic facts.
517The category $\K(\ca)$ satisfies the [TR5] and [TR$5^*$] conditions;
518that is, it has small products and coproducts. The subcategory $\Ac(\ca)$ is \textit{localizing} and
519\textit{colocalizing}, meaning that it is closed under both coproducts and products.
520(In a triangulated category with coproducts, every triangulated subcategory which is
521closed under coproducts is automatically thick by \cite[Proposition~1.6.8]{Neeman99};
522that is, it contains all direct summands of its objects.)
523The derived category of $\ca$ is the Verdier quotient
524\[
525\D(\ca)=\K(\ca)/\Ac(\ca).
526\]
527Since $\Hom_{\D(\ca)}(\zz,\T\zz)\cong\Ext^1_\ca(\zz,\zz)$, Lemma~\ref{L1.3}
528implies the following.
529\cor{C1.4}
530There is a proper class of morphisms $\zz\la\T\zz$ in $\D(\ca)$.
531\qed
532\ecor
533We remark that it does not help if we restrict attention to bounded derived categories,
534since the category $\D^b(\ca)$ does not have small $\Hom$-sets either.
535Recall that a \textit{Bousfield localization} for the pair $\Ac(\ca)\subset\K(\ca)$
536is a right adjoint of the canonical functor $\K(\ca)\la\D(\ca)$, and a
537\textit{Bousfield colocalization} is a left adjoint.
538As shown in \cite[Proposition~9.1.18]{Neeman99}, a Bousfield localization exists for the pair $\Ac(\ca)\subset\K(\ca)$
539if and only if
540the inclusion \[ i\colon\Ac(\ca)\la\K(\ca) \] has a right adjoint.
541Dually, a colocalization exists
542if and only if
543$i$ has a left adjoint.
544\cor{C1.6}
545There is neither a Bousfield localization
546nor a Bousfield colocalization for $\Ac(\ca)$ in $\K(\ca)$.
547The inclusion functor $i\colon\Ac(\ca)\la\K(\ca)$ has neither a right
548adjoint nor a left adjoint.
549\ecor

561Let $\ab$ denote the category of (small) abelian groups.

563if it takes triangles to long exact sequences.
564A triangulated category $\ct$ \textit{satisfies Brown representability}

594from $\K(\ca)$ to~$\ab$. The composite
595\begin{equation}
596\label{composite}
597\CD
598\Ac(\ca) @>i>> \K(\ca) @>\Hom_{\K(\ca)}^{}(\zz,-)>> \ab
599\endCD
600\end{equation}
601is a homological functor taking products
602to products, and we assert that it is not representable
603by any object of~$\Ac(\ca)$.

605If the composite (\ref{composite}) were representable,
606then
607there would exist a map $\ph\colon\zz\la A$ where $A\in\Ac(\ca)$ and such that all other maps from $\zz$ to acyclic complexes factor uniquely through $\ph$. Let us complete $\ph$ to a triangle \[ \CD X @>\alpha>> \zz @>\ph>> A @>>> \T X \endCD \] in $\K(\ca)$.
608Now any morphism $\zz\la\T\zz$ in $\D(\ca)$ can be realized
609as a pair of maps
610\begin{equation}
611\label{pair}
612\xymatrix@C+20pt@R-20pt{
613& Y\ar_{\beta}[dl]\ar[dr] & \\ \zz & & \T\zz
614}
615\end{equation}
616where $\beta$ is a quasi-isomorphism.
617This fits into a triangle \[ \CD Y @>\beta>> \zz @>\psi>> B @>>> \T Y \endCD \] with $B\in\Ac(\ca)$.
618Hence,
619$\psi$ would factor through the universal map $\ph\colon \zz\la A$, and
