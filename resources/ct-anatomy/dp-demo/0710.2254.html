117\section{Statement of previous error}
118In previous work, we studied various kinds of functors $X \colon \Deltaop \rightarrow \SSets$ satisfying a Segal condition, so that the maps
119\[ X_n \rightarrow \underbrace{X_1 \times_{X_0} \cdots \times_{X_0} X_1}_n \] are weak equivalences of simplicial sets for $n \geq 2$.
120When we imposed the additional condition that $X_0=\Delta[0]$, such objects, called \emph{reduced Segal categories} or \emph{Segal monoids} were shown to be
121equivalent to
122simplicial monoids \cite{simpmon}.
123When instead $X_0 =\amalg \Delta[0]$, some discrete simplicial set, then $X$ is a \emph{Segal category} can more generally be regarded as an up-to-homotopy model for a simplicial category with this same object set \cite{simpmon}.
124These results were used in the comparison between the model structure for all Segal categories (not just with a fixed set in degree zero) and the model structure for simplicial categories \cite{thesis}.
125Furthermore, the former model structure was shown to be Quillen
126equivalent to
127the model structure for complete Segal spaces, or functors $\Deltaop \rightarrow \SSets$ where the discrete level zero condition is replaced with a ``completeness" condition \cite{thesis}.
128A natural question was then whether these results could be generalized to an ``invertible" and two methods were proposed in both of the papers \cite{inverses}, \cite{inverses2}. The first was to replace $\Deltaop$ in the above definitions with a category $\IDeltaop$ in which the objects had an involution map. However, these results were in fact incorrect, in that this involution does not adequately encode an inverse map. In this note, we clarify that this diagram should encode the structure of a monoid with involution rather than a group, or category with involution rather than a groupoid, in the case of multiple objects.
129The second approach given in these papers, given by using different projection maps as first used by Bousfield \cite{bous}, is still correct.

133\section{Replacing groups with monoids with involution}
134In the case of monoids, we consider functors $\Deltaop \rightarrow
135\SSets$, where the category $\Deltaop$ has as objects finite
136ordered sets $[n]=(0 \rightarrow 1 \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow
137n)$ for each $n \geq 0$ and as morphisms the opposites of the
138order-preserving maps between them. Notice that each $[n]$ can be
139regarded as a category with $n+1$ objects and a single morphism $i
140\rightarrow j$ whenever $i \leq j$.
141In \cite{inverses} we defined a category $\IDeltaop$,
142whose objects are given by small groupoids $I[n]=(0
143\rightleftarrows 1 \leftrightarrows \cdots \rightleftarrows n)$
144for $n \geq 0$. In other words, each $I[n]$ is a category with
145$n+1$ objects and a single isomorphism between any two objects.
146The morphisms of $\IDeltaop$ are generated by two sets of maps:
147the opposite of the order-preserving maps from
148$\Deltaop$, and an involution morphism on each $I[n]$ which
149sends each $i$ to $n-i$.
150The hope was that functors $\IDeltaop \rightarrow \SSets$ satisfying a Segal condition encoded a group structure. Unfortunately, inverses are not adequately given, so such functors actually give the structure of a monoid with involution. Thus, we change the terminology given in \cite{inverses} as follows.
151In the case of ${\bf \Delta}$, the simplicial set $\Delta[n]$ is
152given by the representable functor $\Hom_{\bf \Delta}(-, [n])$.
153Similarly, we can define an object $I\Delta[n]$ which is
154given by the representable functor $\Hom_{\bf I\Delta}(-, I[n])$.
155These $n$-\emph{simplices with involution} are the standard building blocks
156of the spaces we consider here. In particular, every simplex
157should be regarded as having a corresponding ``reverse" simplex. As
158with simplicial sets, we can consider the boundary of $I\Delta
159[n]$, denoted $\partial I \Delta [n]$, which consists of the nondegenerate simplices of
160$I \Delta [n]$ of degree less than $n$.
161Thus, we can define an \emph{simplicial set with involution} to be a
162functor $\IDeltaop \rightarrow \Sets$ and, more generally, an
163\emph{simplicial object with involution} in a category $\mathcal C$ to
164be a functor $\IDeltaop \rightarrow \mathcal C$. We denote the
165category of simplicial sets with involution by $I\SSets$. We further
166consider the case of simplicial spaces with involution, or functors
167$\IDeltaop \rightarrow \SSets$. Since there is a forgetful functor
168$U:I\SSets \rightarrow \SSets$ (respectively,
169$U:\SSets^{I\Deltaop} \rightarrow \SSets^{\Deltaop}$), we define a
170map $f$ of simplicial sets (respectively, spaces) with involution to be
171a weak equivalence if $U(f)$ is a weak equivalence of simplicial
172sets (respectively, spaces).
173In particular, we define a \emph{Segal precategory with involution} to be an simplicial space with involution $X$ such that the simplicial set $X_0$
174is discrete.
175If $X_0 =\Delta[0]$,
176then
177we call it a \emph{Segal premonoid with involution}.
178To define a Segal category with involution, use the maps \[ \xi_n: X_n \rightarrow \underbrace{X_1 \times_{X_0} \cdots \times_{X_0} X_1}_n \] defined in \cite[\S 4]{inverses}.
179Thus,
180a \emph{Segal category with involution} is a Segal precategory with involution $X$ such that
181for each $n \geq 2$
182the map $\xi_n$ is a weak equivalence of simplicial sets.
183Obtaining an appropriate model structure requires localization with respect to the following map:
184\[ \xi_\mathcal O = \coprod_{n \geq 1}(\xi^n: \coprod_{\xu \in \mathcal O^{n+1}} (IG(n)^t_\xu \rightarrow I\Delta [n]^t_\xu)). \]
185The proofs of the following two propositions continue to hold, with the necessary changes in terminology.
186\begin{prop} \cite[4.1]{inverses}
187There is a model category structure $\LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{\mathcal O,f}$ on the category of
188Segal precategories with involution with a fixed set $\mathcal O$ in degree zero in
189which the weak equivalences and fibrations are given levelwise.
190Similarly, there is a model category structure $\LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{\mathcal O,c}$ on the
191same underlying category in which the weak equivalences and
192cofibrations are given levelwise. Furthermore, we can localize
193each of these model category structures with the map $\xi_\mathcal
194O$ to obtain model structures $\LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{\mathcal O,f}$ and $\LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{\mathcal O,c}$ whose
195fibrant objects are Segal categories with involution.
196\end{prop}
197\begin{prop} \cite[4.2]{inverses}
198The adjoint pair given by the identity functor induces a Quillen
199equivalence of model categories
200\[ \xymatrix@1{\LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{\mathcal O,f} \ar@<.5ex>[r] & \LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{\mathcal O,c}. \ar@<.5ex>[l]} \]
201\end{prop}
202In \cite{inverses}, we claimed that there was a Quillen equivalence
203\[ \LSSetstg_* \leftrightarrows \LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{*,f}. \] Unfortunately, we did not adequately establish that we obtained group structures using this category $\IDeltaop$. We seek to establish that our previous proof instead gave a Quillen equivalence
204\[ \LSSetstmi_* \leftrightarrows \LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{*,f} \] where $\Tmi$ is the \emph{theory of monoids with involution}.
205This theory $\Tmi$ has as objects $T_k$ which are given by the free monoid with involution on $k$ generators. In other words, $T_k$ is free on generators $x_1, \ldots, x_k$ and $\overline{x}_1, \ldots, \overline{x}_k$ with involution $I(x_k)=\overline{x}_k$. There is a monoid map $\tau \colon T_k \rightarrow T_k$ given by $x_j \mapsto \overline{x}_{k-j+1}$. This map will correspond to the flip map of each object $I[k]$ in $\IDeltaop$.
206Using Badzioch's theorem from \cite{bad}, such a Quillen equivalence will complete the proof of the following theorem.
207\begin{theorem}
208\label{main} The model category structure $\Algtmi$ is Quillen
209equivalent to
210the model category structure $\LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{*,f}$.
211\end{theorem}
212As in \cite{simpmon}, we prove this theorem using several lemmas.
213Note that in the model structure $\LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{*,c}$, we denote by
214$L_1$ the localization, or fibrant replacement functor.
215Analogously, we denote by $L_2$ the localization functor in
216$\LSSetstmi_*$.
217The first step in the proof of the theorem is to show what the
218localization functor $L_1$ does to the $n$-simplex with involution
219$I\Delta[n]^t$. By $I\nerve(-)^t$, we denote the representable
220functor $\Hom(I[n],-)$, viewed as a transposed constant simplicial space. It is here, Lemma \cite[4.5]{inverses}, that the major error occurred. The correct statement is as follows.
221\begin{prop} \label{nerve}
222Let $F_n$ denote the free monoid with involution on $n$ generators.
223Then in $\LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{*,c}$, $L_1 I\Delta [n]^t_*$ is weakly
224equivalent to
225$I\nerve (F_n)^t$
226for each $n \geq 0$.
227\end{prop}
228In the proof, we defined a filtration of $I\nerve(F_1)^t$ as follows:
229\[ \Psi_k (I\nerve(F_1)^t)_j=\left\{(x^{n_1}|\cdots |x^{n_j}) \mid \sum_{\ell=1}^j |n_\ell| \leq k \right\} \]
230where $x$ and its ``inverse" $x^{-1}$ denote the two nondegenerate 1-simplices of $I \Delta[1]^t_*= \Psi_1$. The problem is that we assumed here that $x$ could be canceled with $x^{-1}$, when there is no structure built in to $\IDeltaop$ to make this cancelation possible. In short, we do not really have the structure of a group, but only of a monoid with involution. Therefore, we instead define the filtration of $I\nerve(F_1)^t$ by
231\[ \Psi_k (I\nerve(F_1)^t)_j=\left\{(w_1|\cdots |w_j) \mid \ell(w_1 \cdots w_j) \leq k \right\} \] where the $w_i$ are words in $x$ and $\overline{x}$, and $\ell$ denotes word length.
232With this modification, the previous proof goes through as before.
233To obtain the appropriate adjoint pair for the Quillen equivalence, we first define a functor $J \colon \IDeltaop \rightarrow \Tmi$. On objects, this functor is defined by $I{n} \mapsto T_n$. On face and degeneracy maps coming from $\Deltaop$, this functor behaves the same as the functor $\Deltaop \rightarrow \Tm$ as defined in \cite{simpmon}. Specifically, the coface and codegeneracy maps maps in ${\bf I\Delta}$ are given by
234\[ d^i(x_k)=
235\begin{cases}
236x_k & i>k \\
237x_kx_{k+1} & i=k \\
238x_{k+1} & i<k
239\end{cases} \text{ and }
240s^i(x_k)=
241\begin{cases}
242x_k & i \leq k \\
243e & i=k-1 \\
244x_{k-1} & i<k-1.
245\end{cases} \]
246The involution map is sent to the map $\tau \colon T_\ell \rightarrow T_\ell$ defined by $x_k \mapsto y_{\ell-k+1}$.
247This functor $J$ induces a map
248\[ J^\ast \colon \LSSetstmi_{\ast,f} \rightarrow \LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{\ast,f} \] for which we have a left adjoint $J_\ast$ via left Kan extension.
249As in \cite{inverses}, define $IM[k]$ to be the functor $\Tmi \rightarrow \SSets$ given by
250\[ F_n \mapsto \Hom_{\Tmi}(F_k, F_n)=(F_k)^n, \] and let $H = I\nerve(F_k)^t$. The following results continue to hold, replacing $\Tg$ with $\Tmi$.
251\begin{lemma}
252\cite[4.9]{inverses} In $\LSSets^{\Tmi}_\ast$, $L_2J_*(H)$ is weakly
253equivalent to
254$IM[k]$.
255\end{lemma}
256\begin{prop} \cite[4.10]{inverses}
257For any object $X$ in $\LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{\ast, c}$, $L_1X$ is weakly
258equivalent to
259$J^*L_2J_*X$.
260\end{prop}
261\begin{prop} \cite[4.11, 4.3]{inverses}
262The adjoint pair
263\[ \xymatrix@1{J_*: \LSSets^{\IDeltaop}_{*,f} \ar@<.5ex>[r] & \SSets^{\Tmi}_* :J^* \ar@<.5ex>[l]} \]
264is a Quillen equivalence.
265\end{prop}
266The more general fixed-object case, as well as the more general case as developed in \cite{inverses2}, can be corrected by considering categories with involution rather than groupoids whenever $\IDeltaop$ is the indexing category.
