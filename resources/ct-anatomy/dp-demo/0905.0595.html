59{Are all cofibrantly generated model categories combinatorial?}

69\begin{abstract}
70G.
71Raptis has recently proved that, assuming Vop\v enka's principle, every cofibrantly generated model category is Quillen
72equivalent to
73a combinatorial one.
74His result remains true for a slightly more general concept
75of a cofibrantly generated model category.
76We show that Vop\v enka's principle
77is equivalent to
78this claim.
79The set-theoretical status of the original Raptis' result is open.
80\end{abstract}
81\keywords{cofibrantly generated model category, combinatorial model category, Vop\v enka's principle}
82\maketitle
83Combinatorial model categories were introduced by J. H. Smith as model categories which are locally presentable
84and cofibrantly generated. There are of course cofibrantly generated model categories which are not combinatorial
85-- the first example is the standard model category of topological spaces. This model category is Quillen equivalent
86to the combinatorial model category of simplicial sets. G. Raptis \cite{R} has recently proved a somewhat surprising
87result saying that, assuming Vop\v enka's principle, every cofibrantly generated model category is Quillen equivalent
88to a combinatorial model category. Vop\v enka's principle is a set-theoretical axiom implying the existence of very
89large cardinals (see \cite{AR}). A natural question is whether Vop\v enka's principle (or other set theory) is needed
90for Raptis' result.
91A \textit{model category} is a complete and cocomplete category $\cm$ together with three classes of
92morphisms $\cf$, $\cc$ and $\cw$ called \textit{fibrations}, \textit{cofibrations} and \textit{weak
93equivalences} such that
94\begin{enumerate}
95\item[(1)] $\cw$ has the 2-out-of-3 property and is closed under retracts in the arrow category $\cm^\to$, and
96\item[(2)] $(\cc,\cf\cap\cw)$ and $(\cc\cap\cw,\cf)$ are weak factorization systems.
97\end{enumerate}
98Morphisms from $\cf\cap\cw$ are called \textit{trivial fibrations} while morphisms from $\cc\cap\cw$
99\textit{trivial cofibrations}.
100A \textit{weak factorization system} $(\cl,\crr)$ in a category $\cm$ consists of two classes $\cl$ and $\crr$
101of morphisms of $\cm$ such that
102\begin{enumerate}
103\item[(1)] $\crr = \cl^{\square}$, $\cl = {}^\square \crr$, and
104\item[(2)] any morphism $h$ of $\cm$ has a factorization $h=gf$ with
105$f\in \cl$ and $g\in \crr$.
106\end{enumerate}
107Here, $\cl^{\square}$ consists of morphisms having the right lifting property w.r.t. each morphism from $\cl$
108and ${}^\square\crr$ consists of morphisms having the left lifting property w.r.t. each morphism from $\crr$.
109The standard definition of a cofibrantly generated model category (see \cite{Ho}) is that the both weak factorization
110systems from its definition are cofibrantly generated in the following sense. A weak factorization system $(\cl,\crr)$
111is cofibrantly generated if there exists a set $\cx$ of morphisms such that
112\begin{enumerate}
113\item[(1)] the domains of $\cx$ are small relative to $\cx$-cellular morphisms, and
114\item[(2)] $\cx^\square=\crr$.
115\end{enumerate}
116Here, $\cx$-cellular morphisms are transfinite compositions of pushouts of morphisms of $\cx$. The consequence
117of this definition is that $\cl$ is the smallest cofibrantly closed class containing $\cx$. A cofibrantly closed
118class is defined as a class of morphisms closed under transfinite compositions, pushouts and retracts in $\cm^\to$.
119Moreover, one does not need to assume that $(\cl,\crr)$ is a weak factorization system because it follows from (1)
120and (2). This observation led to the following more general definition of a cofibrantly generated weak factorization
121system (see \cite{AHRT}).
122A weak factorization system $(\cl,\crr)$ is \textit{cofibrantly generated} if there exists a set $\cx$ of morphisms
123such that $\cl$ is the smallest cofibrantly closed class containing $\cx$. The consequence is that $\cx^\square=\crr$.
124A model category is \textit{cofibrantly generated} if the both weak factorization systems from its definition
125are cofibrantly generated in the new sense. It does not affect the definition of a combinatorial model category
126because all objects are small in a locally presentable category. Moreover, the proof of Raptis \cite{R} works
127for cofibrantly generated model categories in this sense as well.
128We will show that Vop\v enka's principle follows from the fact that
129every cofibrantly generated model category (in the new sense) is Quillen
130equivalent to
131a combinatorial model category.
132We do not know whether this is true
133for standardly defined cofibrantly generated model categories as well. Our proof uses the trivial model structure
134on a category $\cm$ where all morphisms are cofibrations and weak equivalences are isomorphisms.
135Given a small full subcategory $\ca$ of a category $\ck$, the \textit{canonical functor}
136$$
137E_\ca: \ck \to \Set^{\ca^{\op}}
138$$
139assigns to each object $K$ the restriction
140$$
141E_\ca K =\hom (-, K)\big/\ca^{op}
142$$
143of its hom-functor $\hom(-, K): \ck^{\op}\to \Set$ to $\ca^{\op}$ (see \cite{AR} 1.25).
144A small full subcategory $\ca$ of a category $\ck$ is called \textit{dense} provided that every object of $\ck$
145is a canonical colimit of objects from $\ca$. It is equivalent to the fact that the canonical functor
146$$
147E_\ca: \ck \to \Set^{\ca^{\op}}
148$$
149is a full embedding (see \cite{AR}, 1.26). A category $\ck$ is called \textit{bounded} if it has a (small) dense
150subcategory (see \cite{AR}).
151Dense subcategories were introduced by J. R. Isbell \cite{I} and called left adequate subcategories. The following
152result is easy to prove and can be found in \cite{I}.
153\begin{lemma}\label{le0.1}
154Let $\ca$ be dense subcategory of $\ck$ and $\cb$ a small full subcategory of $\ck$ containing $\ca$. Then $\cb$
155is dense.
156\end{lemma}
157\begin{propo}\label{prop0.2}
158Let $\ck$ be a cocomplete bounded category. Then $(\ck,\Iso)$ is a cofibrantly generated weak factorization system.
159\end{propo}
160\begin{proof}
161Clearly, $(\ck,\Iso)$ is a weak factorization system. The canonical functor
162$$
163E_\ca: \ck \to \Set^{\ca^{\op}}
164$$
165has a left adjoint $F$ (see \cite{AR}, 1.27).
166The weak factorization system $(\Set^{\ca^{\op}},\Iso)$ in $\Set^{\ca^{\op}}$ is cofibrantly generated (see \cite{Ro1}, 4.6).
167Thus
168there is a small full subcategory $\cx$ of $\Set^{\ca^{\op}}$ such that each morphism in $\Set^{\ca^{\op}}$ is a retract of a $\cx$-cellular morphism.
169Hence
170each morphism in $\ck$ is a retract of a $F(\cx)$-cellular morphism.
171Thus
172$(\ck,\Iso)$ is cofibrantly generated.
173\end{proof}
174Given a complete and cocomplete category $\ck$, the choice $\cc=\ck$ and $\cw=\Iso$ yields a model category structure
175on $\ck$. The corresponding two weak factorization systems are $(\ck,\Iso)$ and $(\Iso,\ck)$ and the homotopy category
176$\Ho(\ck)=\ck$. We will call this model category structure \textit{trivial}.
177\begin{coro}\label{cor0.3}
178Let $\ck$ be a complete, cocomplete and bounded category. Then the trivial model category structure on $\ck$
179is cofibrantly generated.
180\end{coro}
181\begin{proof}
182Following \ref{prop0.2}, it suffices to add that the weak factorization system $(\Iso,\ck)$ is cofibrantly generated
183by $\cx=\{\id_O\}$ where $O$ is an initial object of $\ck$.
184\end{proof}
185\begin{theo}
186\label{th0.4} Vop\v enka's principle
187is equivalent to
188the fact that every cofibrantly generated model category is Quillen equivalent to a combinatorial model category.
189\end{theo}
190\begin{proof}
191Necessity follows from \cite{R}. Under the negation of Vop\v enka's principle, \cite{AR}, 6.12 presents a complete
192bounded category $\ca$ with
193the following properties
194\begin{enumerate}
195\item[(1)] For each regular cardinal $\lambda$, there is a $\lambda$-filtered diagram $D_\lambda:\cd_\lambda\to\ck$
196whose only compatible cocones $\delta_\lambda$ are trivial ones with the codomain 1 (= a terminal object),
197\item[(2)] For each $\lambda$, $\id_1$ does not factorize through any component of $\delta_\lambda$.
198\end{enumerate}
199Since,
200following (1),
201$\delta_\lambda$ is a colimit cocone for each $\lambda$
202, (2)
203implies that
204$1$ is not $\lambda$-presentable for any regular $\lambda$.
205Condition (2) is not stated explicitly in \cite{AR} but it
206follows from the fact that
207there is no morphism from $1$ to a non-terminal object of $\ca$.
208In fact,
209$\ca$ is the full subcategory of the category $\Gra$ consisting of graphs $A$ without any morphism $B_i\to A$
210where $B_i$ is the rigid class of graphs indexed by ordinals (whose existence is guaranteed by the negation
211of Vop\v enka's principle).
212The existence of a morphism $1\to A$
213means
214the presence of a loop in $A$ and,
215consequently
216, the existence of a constant morphism $B_i\to A$ (having a loop as its value).
217Assume that the trivial model category $\ca$ is Quillen
218equivalent to
219a combinatorial model category $\cm$.
220Since $\Ho\cm$
221is equivalent to
222$\ca$, it shares properties (1)
223and (2). Moreover, since $\Ho\ck=\ck$,
224the diagrams $D_\lambda$ are diagrams in $\ck$. It follows from the definition of Quillen equivalence
225that the corresponding diagrams in $\Ho\cm$ (we will denote them by $D_\lambda$ as well) can be rectified.
226It means that there are diagrams $\overline{D}_\lambda$ in $\cm$ such that $D_\lambda=P\overline{D}_\lambda$;
227here, $P:\cm\to\Ho\cm$ is the canonical functor. Following \cite{D} and \cite{Ro}, there is a regular cardinal
228$\lambda_0$ such that the replacement functor $R:\cm\to\cm$ preserves $\lambda_0$-filtered colimits. $R$ sends
229each object $M$ to a fibrant and cofibrant object and the canonical functor $P$ can be taken as the composition
230$QR$ where $Q$ is the quotient functor identifying homotopy equivalent morphisms.
231Let
232$$
233(\overline{\delta}_{\lambda d}:\overline{D}_\lambda d\to M_\lambda)_{d\in\cd_\lambda}
234$$
235be colimit cocones. Then
236$$
237(R\overline{\delta}_{\lambda d}:R\overline{D}_\lambda d\to RM_\lambda)_{d\in\cd_\lambda}
238$$
239are colimit cocones for each $\lambda>\lambda_0$. Following (1), $RM_\lambda\cong 1$ for each $\lambda>\lambda_0$.
240The object $RM_{\lambda_0}$ is $\mu$-presentable in $\cm$ for some regular cardinal $\lambda_0<\mu$. Since
241$RM_{\lambda_0}$ and $RM_\mu$ are homotopy equivalent, there is a morphism $f:RM_{\lambda_0}\to RM_\mu$. Since
242$f$ factorizes through some $R\overline{\delta}_{\mu d}$, $\id_1$ factorizes through some component
243of $\delta_\mu$, which contradicts (2).
244\end{proof}
245While the weak factorization system $(\Iso,\ck)$ is cofibrantly generated in the sense of \cite{Ho}, it is not true
246for $(\ck,\Iso)$ because the complete, cocomplete and bounded category in \cite{AR}, 6.12 is not locally
247presentable just because it contains a non-presentable object. Thus we do not know whether Vop\v enka's principle
248follows from the original result from \cite{R}.
249The proof above does not exclude that $\ca$ \textit{has a combinatorial model}, i.e., that there is a combinatorial model category $\cm$ such that $\ca$
250is equivalent to
251$\Ho\cm$.
252\begin{propo}\label{prop0.4} Assume the existence of a proper class of compact cardinals and let $\ck$ be a complete,
253cocomplete and bounded category.
254Then the trivial model category $\ck$ has a combinatorial model
255if and only if
256$\ck$ is locally presentable.
257\end{propo}
258\begin{proof}
259If $\ck$ is locally presentable the trivial model category $\ck$ is combinatorial.
260Assume that the trivial model category $\ck$
261is equivalent to
262$\Ho\cm$ where $\cm$ is a combinatorial model category.
263Let $\cx$ be a dense
264subcategory of $\ck$. Following \cite{Ro}, 4.1, there is a regular cardinal $\lambda$ such that
265\begin{enumerate}
266\item[(1)] $\cx\subseteq P(\cm_\lambda)$ where $\cm_\lambda$ denotes the full subcategory of $\cm$ consisting
267of $\lambda$-presentable objects,
268\item[(2)] The composition $H=E_{P(\cm_\lambda)}\cdot P$ preserves $\lambda$-filtered colimits.
269\end{enumerate}
270Since $P(\cm_\lambda)$ is dense in $\ck$ (see \ref{le0.1}), $E_{P(\cm_\lambda)}$ is a full embedding.
271Hence
272$\ck$ is the full image of the functor $H$, i.e., the full subcategory on objects $H(M)$ with $M$ in $\cm$.
273Following
274\cite{Ro2}, Corollary of Theorem 2, $\ck$ is locally presentable.
275\end{proof}
276Vop\v enka's principle is stronger than the existence of a proper class of compact cardinals.
277Thus,
278assuming the negation of Vop\v enka's principle but the existence of a proper class of compact cardinals, there is a cofibrantly generated model category without a combinatorial model.
