47Let $\ev = Vect_{k}$ (finite dimensional $k$-vector spaces) and let $\ba$ be a finite representable $k$-linear flock (i.e. a finite $\ev$-herd): thus the objects of $\ba$ are finite in number, $\ba ( a, b)$ finite, $\ba(a,b)^{\ast} \cong \ba(b,a)$ chosen
48(we refer the reader to [1] for the definition of a $k$-linear flock).
49\noindent Then the category
50$$[\ba\op\otimes\ba , Vect_{k}] $$
51\noindent of finite bimodules over $\ba$ is \emph{$\ast$-autonomous} (as shown in [3]).
52\noindent
53If also $$\ba(b, \tau(a,c,d))^{\ast} \cong \ba(a,\tau (b,d,c))$$ \noindent
54then
55we obtain a functor (i.e. antipode)
56\begin{eqnarray*}S: & \bh\op \longrightarrow \bh \\ & (x,y) \mapsto (y,x)\end{eqnarray*}
57\noindent where the objects of $\bh$ are the pairs $(a,b)\in \ba\op\otimes\ba$ and the hom of $\bh$ is
58$$\bh((a,b),(c,d))=\ba(\tau(b,a,c),d)\,\, .$$
59\noindent Moreover, the convolution $[\bh,Vect_{k}]$ with respect to the promonoidal structure on $\bh$ given by
60\begin{equation}p((a,b),(c,d),(u,v)) = \ba(\tau(d,c,b),v)\otimes\ba(u,a)\end{equation}
61$$j(u,v) = \ba(u,v)$$
62\noindent is $\ast$-autonomous for the above $S$.
63There is a prospective Fourier transformation functor $\hat{K}$ on $[\bh,Vect_{k}]$ given by
64$$\hat{K}(f)(a,b) = \int\limits^{(x,y)\in\bh} f(x,y)\otimes\ba(\tau(y,x,a),b)$$
65\noindent where
66$$\xymatrix{[\bh,Vect_{k}] \ar@<+1ex>[rr]^{\hat{K}} \ar@<-1ex>@{<-}[rr]_{\check{K}} && [\ba\op\otimes\ba,Vect_{k}]}$$
67\noindent with $\hat{K}\dashv\check{K}$.
68The cocontinuous functor $\hat{K}$ is certainly multiplicative and preserves the induced duality on $[\bh,Vect_{f}]$ defined by $f^{\ast}(x,y) = f(y,x)^{\ast}$.
69Further, $\hat{K}$ is conservative (as required in [2]).
70\pagebreak
71In fact the Fourier functor $\hat{K}$ is here nothing other than the process of ``restriction along $h$'' where
72$$h:\ba\op\otimes\ba\to\bh$$
73is the canonical Kleisli functor mapping $(a,b)\in\ba\op\otimes\ba$ to $(a,b)\in\bh$, which is surjective on objects (see [1] for the details of this construction).
74The functor $\hat{K}=[h,1]$ also preserves both the left and right internal homs of the convolution structure on $[\bh , Vect_{k}]$, this is a straight forward calculation.
75So
76$[\bh , Vect_{k}]$ is a $\ast$-autonomous monoidal biclosed category because $[\ba\op\otimes\ba, Vect_{k}]$ is such (and $\hat{K}=[h,1]$ is conservative).
77The $\ast$-autonomy of $[\ba\op\otimes\ba, Vect_{k}]$ also
78implies that
79the domain $\bh$ is itself $\ast$-autonomous as a promonoidal category over $Vect_{k}$, that is, the promultiplication $p$ on $\bh$ mentioned above (1) satisfies the cyclic relations given in [2]
80and [3] for example. \newline
81Thus
82$\hat{K}=[h,1]$ is a quantum groupoid in the sense of [3] (see also [5]), and there is undoubtedly also a connection with the article by T.
83Brzezi\'nski and J. Vercruysse: ``Bimodule herds'', J. Alg. 321 (2009), 2670-2704.
84Indeed, the Kleisli functor (or ``algebroid'')
85$$h:\ba\op\otimes\ba\to\bh$$
86may be related to the Ehresmann Groupoid, even in the more general case where $\ba$ is not necessarily finite as a $\ev$-herd.
